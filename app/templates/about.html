The Network

{{ intro }}

Every aircraft carries a transponder that announces its identity, position, altitude and speed on 1090 MHz (ADS-B), and many small aircraft use 978 MHz (UAT). Each station in the network receives those broadcasts with an inexpensive receiver and sends them to a central hub, which merges them into one statewide picture. Where stations overlap, coverage is better, and when three or more stations hear the same aircraft the network can even work out the position of aircraft that do not broadcast one (MLAT). Where there are gaps, a new station fills one.

Want to add a station of your own? Start with How to Feed. Questions? Contact us.

Where It Is Going

The aircraft network was the first part. Members' weather stations, radiosonde (weather balloon) tracking and the county map are here now. Next are a repeater directory, APRS and mesh radio nodes, so one account and one set of maps cover what is overhead, outdoors and on the air. The Roadmap shows what is being built now.

Your Privacy

Station owners decide how precisely their location is shown: by default it is rounded to roughly {{ 10|u_dist }}, and each owner can choose anything from exact through twelve steps of rounding to hidden. The public pages show station names and statistics, never home addresses. Aircraft data is public radio traffic.

The site uses a sign-in cookie for members and stores your display choices (theme, clock and time zone) in your browser or account. It does not use advertising or tracking cookies, and aircraft lookups are made by the portal on your behalf, so the outside services do not see which aircraft you looked at.
